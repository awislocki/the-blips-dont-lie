## The Docket

### Improving Social Security's Service to Victims of Identity Theft Act — PLAW-119publ119

*(H.R. 5345; passed House Dec. 1, 2025, passed Senate Sept. 17, 2026; approved Oct. 7, 2026. Full 2-page text read.)*

Amends Title VII of the Social Security Act to require the SSA Commissioner to set up a "single point of contact" — a team of specially trained employees — for anyone whose Social Security number has been misused (fraudulent benefit claims, corrupted records, forced reissuance) or whose card was lost in the mail. That team must track the person's case to resolution, coordinate across SSA units, maintain continuity of records, and notify the individual as appropriate. Takes effect 180 days after enactment. Affects identity-theft victims dealing with SSA and the agency's internal case-routing procedures.

- **THE GOOD:** Targets a documented, specific pain point — victims bounced between SSA units with no single owner of their case. Requires continuity of records and victim notification, not just a vague promise to "help."
- **THE BAD:** No staffing or funding is authorized, no case-resolution timeline is set beyond "as quickly as possible," and there's no reporting requirement to Congress or the public to verify SSA actually implements this. The "team or subset" of employees can rotate freely, which cuts against the stated goal of accountability.
- **THE UGLY:** Nothing found — a clean 2-page amendment that does exactly what its title says, no more.
- **Grade: C+.** Sound, narrow fix; undermined by having no way for anyone outside SSA to check whether it worked.

*(Issued 2026-10-15. Text also unavailable after one retry; metadata only.)*
